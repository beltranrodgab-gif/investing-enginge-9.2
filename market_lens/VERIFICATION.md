# Verification — evidence, not preloaded data

**Live collection.** We started the actual scheduled worker on an empty database. All 26 public requests were accepted: ten distinct institutions, 154 defined series. The replay of those responses into an empty journal produced 10,372 series/period cells across all 154 series. These include historical cells and calculated values; they are **not** newly published releases. We independently decompressed and checked each stored body's SHA-256, reparsed every raw body and matched every normalized observation to SQLite. A full worker restart kept all receipts and the original next-due times, without hitting the sources again before they were due.

**72 numeric latest values, one intentionally blank.** The BLS response included an October US CPI level but not the corresponding year-earlier October level; the exact 12-month change was therefore withheld. The latest available numeric YoY and its own period are shown separately. Five Eurostat unemployment series ended at their latest *reported* month, not an empty month label in the common time axis. The receipt clock in this demonstration was the environment's November 20 UTC clock. None of these local receipts is claimed as of the November 18 archive cutoff. Your installation uses its own clock and starts its own journal.

**A timer actually fired.** In an isolated copy of the real journal, we enabled just the ECB feed and set a single near-future QA due time. We started one normal `run` process, with no browser open. It stayed idle until the target, made a second genuine ECB HTTPS request automatically, found the same raw hash and recorded `unchanged` (not a new release). It then scheduled its normal 30-minute interval. Restarting again produced no early duplicate. The near-future test target was not a change to the shipped cadence. No claim is made that a publisher happened to release a new number during this brief test.

**Offline regression.** `python3 -m unittest -v test_auto` runs 196 tests. They exercise all 26 source formats and the scheduler/journal: fixture-driven new period and revision under a single advancing timer, as-of/no look-ahead, settings as-of, early rechecks, missing/withdrawn/stale values, parser failures, host cooldown, crash recovery, singleton lock, CSV/backup checkpoint, and service manifest/heartbeat checks. They also cover the strategy layer: the efficient frontier against the closed-form global minimum-variance solution, caps leaving explicit cash instead of rescaling, exact target-return solves, frontier monotonicity and optimality residuals, Monte Carlo medians against `exp(mu - var/2)`, pathwise/closed-form agreement, the operations ladder meeting every risk budget, plans where `invested + cash` equals the account value, determinism, defensive scaling, the supervisor's change triggers, and the `jarvis` CLI refusing to advise when the journal is absent, reporting regime and risk scale consistently with its own decision, and rejecting out-of-range input; and the paper account marking to market, charging costs, enforcing its turnover budget, labelling fail-closed exits, reporting unpriced holdings, and persisting a resume-able NAV while rejecting corrupt files. The price source is covered offline: adjusted close is preferred over raw close, null prices are skipped rather than filled, malformed payloads are refused with a reason, a cold cache offline fails loudly, a stale cache is used but labelled `STALE`, and volatility is estimated from history while flat series are reported instead of assumed safe. No test in the suite contacts the network — the live Yahoo calls are exercised only by hand. `qa/offline-fixtures.zip` contains pinned publisher responses **for tests only**. The live worker never reads it. To check shipped files: `python3 verify_package.py`.

**Research pipeline, tested live then offline.** The research collector was run against real sources: AAPL stored 11,581 daily bars from 1980 with 98 corporate actions, SPY 8,514 bars from 1993 with 136, SEC returned 25,604 dated facts and 1,005 filings for Apple with a median 34-day filing lag, and all ten cross-asset symbols (including ^MOVE) responded. Results that shaped the code, all measured rather than assumed:

- `range=max` returns a **thinned** series (169 points spanning 1980-2026, no events) instead of full history. Full history is now requested with an epoch window, which returns the real ~11,600 bars with every dividend and split, and a parse-time sparsity check refuses a degraded series rather than storing it. This was a genuine silent-corruption bug, not a cosmetic one.
- Apple retired its `Revenues` XBRL tag in 2018, so a naive lookup returns a 2018 figure forever while looking current. A concept-family resolver now picks the live tag and still respects as-of dates; both behaviours are asserted.
- A 404 from EDGAR's company-facts endpoint is reported as "this issuer tags no us-gaap facts" (normal for ETFs such as SPY) rather than as a network failure.
- FINRA's short-interest endpoint ignores every sort and filter parameter and serves its oldest page (2020). It is stored as dated history with `current: false` and a stated reason, never as current short interest.

The 30 new tests cover point-in-time correctness (a restatement returns the original value as of an earlier date), the retired-concept trap, adjustment factors as-of, bar immutability and re-ingest idempotency, provider corrections being reported rather than applied, malformed payload rejection, the FINRA `999.99` sentinel and stale-row handling, and coverage honesty: an empty store claims nothing, an unavailable dataset is never reported available, a partially-covered cross-asset group names the missing symbol, and macro coverage is not asserted without a readable journal. The command refuses an unknown interval, an invalid ticker and a missing `--init` before touching any database.

**Live quotes, options surface, consensus and delisted issuers.** These were built against sources tested live before any code was written, and the tests encode the specific traps each one has:

| Trap | What happened | What the code now does |
|---|---|---|
| A quote called "NBBO" | Nasdaq answers with real bid/ask **and** sizes, but it is one venue's book | Stored with a `quote_scope` stamp stating it is not consolidated; tested |
| Unverifiable recency | A quote from a previous session looks exactly like a live one | Freshness comes from the venue timestamp; missing or unparseable means **not current**; tested |
| Settlements as `11/13/2026` | Date comparison failed, so **fresh** short interest was reported stale | Normalised to ISO on store; the exact bug is pinned by a test |
| Periods sorted as strings | `Dec 2027` sorted before `Jun 2027` | A sortable `period_key` is derived and used for ordering; tested |
| One page of a chain | SPY stopped at strike 676 with spot at 718 — the money was absent, so *every* IV failed and it looked like a market problem | Pagination follows `offset` to completion, with de-duplication; tested against a two-page fixture |
| Inverting in-the-money options | 52 of 134 two-sided Apple call quotes sat fractionally below intrinsic, producing meaningless volatilities | Only the OTM leg is inverted at each strike, and ITM skips are counted; tested |
| A median called "ATM" | The headline IV was a median of all strikes, which is a different number | ATM is interpolated between the strikes bracketing spot, per expiry; tested |
| `25-NSE` as an issuer event | It is filed by the *exchange*, so the candidate list filled with NYSE and Nasdaq | Separated into `exchange_notice` and excluded from issuer attribution; tested |
| EDGAR's description line contains "Company Name" | Header detection picked the description and parsed zero records | The header must carry every column label; tested |
| The form column swallowing the CIK | Form strings read `D/A   1717274`, so **zero** deregistrations matched | Bounded to the fixed-width column; tested |
| Absence from the registrant list | Not evidence of anything on its own | A candidate needs an explicit deregistration form **and** absence; tested |

**Strategy-engine audit.** The data work above was followed by a second pass over the decision code, testing the maths rather than reading it. Seven defects were reproduced numerically, each fixed with a test that asserts the corrected behaviour:

| Defect | What actually happened | What the code now does |
|---|---|---|
| Inverted frequency preference | `FREQ_RANK` is 0 for daily and 3 for annual, but the rank was kept **positive** while the comparison minimised the key — so `min()` chose the *annual* series to represent a group that also held a daily one | The key is negated and compared by maximum; a test asserts daily beats monthly, monthly beats annual, and that an unknown frequency cannot outrank daily |
| Periods compared as raw strings | `'2026Q3'` and `'2026-9-1'` both sort **after** `'2026-11-18'`, so a stale quarterly row could win on recency; the same comparison also drove `group_representatives`, which reported the opposite winner from the live filter | Periods are normalised to an ISO end date (quarter to its last day, month to its last day); an unreadable period sorts before every real date, and the diagnostic uses the same comparison as the filter |
| Regime labels never matched | The detector returns `normal_expansion`, the supervisor table was keyed `normal` — so a healthy economy was described as *"Regime unresolved"*. Separately the table mapped `uncertain` to risk **1.0** while the detector fails closed at **0.5** | One `REGIME_META` table derives both outlook and risk from the detector's own names, with an unknown label falling back to the defensive 0.5; a test asserts the supervisor is never looser than the detector for every reachable regime |
| Correlations scaled twice | A sleeve's `volatility` already includes its `risky_fraction`, and the pairwise product multiplied by both risky fractions again. A halved-risk plan showed an implied correlation of **0.085** against a **0.30** prior — making the book look better diversified than it is | The covariance is `corr · vol_a · vol_b`; a test recovers every one of the 28 pairwise priors exactly at full and half risk |
| Sleeve cash earned 0% | Money held back inside a sleeve to respect its risk budget earned nothing, so a defensive plan was penalised for being defensive — a halved-risk sleeve showed **2.70%** instead of **3.61%** expected return | Held-back cash earns the risk-free rate; a test asserts the blend exactly and that a fully invested sleeve is unchanged |
| Drawdown trigger was dead | `decide()` never emitted a drawdown, and the supervisor read a different key (`drawdown` not `drawdown_pct`), so the limit could not fire on any real decision | `decide()` reports `drawdown_pct` whenever a curve is supplied and the supervisor reads it; a test fires the breach at 20%, stays quiet at 2%, and distinguishes *"supplied but unusable"* from *"none supplied"* |
| A junk curve crashed the decision | The cleaned curve was computed but the guard was still handed the **raw** one, so one non-numeric log row made `max()` raise and took down the whole decision | The guard receives the cleaned curve and needs at least two usable points; a test passes `[-1.0, 'x']` and expects a decision, not an exception |

One smaller correctness fix in the same pass: a sector whose average EV/EBITDA is non-positive used to score the name **0.0**, which is a *middle* rank on no evidence. The name is now dropped, and ties are broken by ticker so ranking is deterministic.

Form 25 turned out to be a much better source than the issuer index alone. The notice names the **issuer** in a separate XML element from the filing exchange — which is precisely why attributing it to the filer had put NYSE and Nasdaq in the delisted-issuer list — and its exhibit states the trading-suspension date and, for mergers, the terminal cash consideration. A live quarter yielded 25 events, 11 with an explicit suspension date. The cash price is taken **only** under the replacement provision (`12d2-2(a)(3)`), because a redemption figure is not a share price; a missing suspension date is stored as absent rather than backfilled from the filing date. Both are tested.

Public float was already inside the EDGAR company-facts payload the collector reads (`dei:EntityPublicFloat`, 20 years for Apple) and had simply never been surfaced; it is now declared `partial` with its fiscal-year-end and dollar-value limits stated, rather than being reported unavailable.

Two limits are stated rather than worked around: the venue serves only the nearest expiries, so this is a short-dated surface and not a term structure; and delisted **prices** remain unobtainable — every price endpoint tested returns 404 for Lehmann, Enron, Bear Stearns, WorldCom, GM and Twitter — so the issuer universe is survivorship-aware while a price backtest is not, and both are said plainly.

**UI and services.** The running monitor was exercised in a browser at desktop and phone widths, including area + measure search, row receipts, feed schedule, empty release journal and a full-catalogue CSV download. Linux service syntax was checked with `systemd-analyze verify`. macOS and Windows service definitions were checked structurally, **not** tested through a real native login/reboot here. Installation checks for a new worker heartbeat; confirm the dashboard stays online after your first login/reboot. Networking policy, a sleeping machine and publisher outages can still interrupt collection.

`verification/baseline_feeds.csv` contains the request, times, SHA-256, count and next due for each actual baseline. `baseline_series.csv` is the matching **example snapshot, not a live file**. `timed_recheck.json` is the second-retrieval receipt; `live_check.json` is the summary.
